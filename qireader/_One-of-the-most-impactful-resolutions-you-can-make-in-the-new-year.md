Throughout the 2010s, eating less meat and embracing plant-based food was — to
many Americans — aspirational.

Large swathes of [the public](https://news.gallup.com/poll/282779/nearly-one-
four-cut-back-eating-meat.aspx) [told
pollsters](https://hub.jhu.edu/2018/09/12/consumers-cut-back-on-meat-
consumption/) they were trying to cut back on meat, lots of schools and
hospitals participated in [Meatless
Monday](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10976850/), [A-list
celebrities](https://www.businessinsider.com/what-does-beyonce-eat-beyonces-
diet-2017-11) dabbled in [veganism](https://www.today.com/food/lizzo-shares-
what-she-eats-day-her-new-vegan-diet-t185597), and venture capital investors
bet big that plant-based meat products, like those from Impossible Foods and
Beyond Meat, were the [next big trend in food](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/2019/10/7/20880318/meatless-meat-mainstream-backlash-impossible-
burger).

And for good reason. People were concerned about what the more than 200 pounds
of meat that Americans eat on average each year does to our health.
[Undercover investigations](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/2019/1/11/18176551/ag-gag-laws-factory-farms-explained) that exposed
the cruelty of factory farms shocked us. And animal agriculture’s [huge
environmental footprint](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/463643/eat-lancet-
plant-based-diet-climate-week) slowly gained attention in the news.

But now, America is “done pretending about meat,” as The Atlantic [put
it](https://www.theatlantic.com/health/archive/2025/03/meat-boom-trump-rfk-
jr/682150/) earlier this year. Plant-based meat sales are declining, some
celebrities are backtracking on their plant-based diets, and the [carnivore
diet](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/466465/hunter-gatherers-meat-myths-
carnivore-paleo-maggots), while still fringe, is ascendant on social media.

I’m not going to suggest I have a neat theory that explains this shift, but I
think a few cultural dynamics explain some of it.

The first is the increasingly pervasive, yet misguided, notion — especially
popular on the political left — that our [individual actions don’t
matter](https://www.sustainabilitybynumbers.com/p/the-false-dichotomy-of-
systemic-and) and that all responsibility to fix social problems lies with
corporations and governments. The second is the rightward, reactionary shift
of [the electorate](https://www.vox.com/politics/414370/2024-election-results-
exit-polls-catalist) and [pop culture](https://www.vox.com/culture/385292/pop-
culture-maga-trump-election-morgan-wallen-post-malone-twisters-zach-bryan).

The third unites people of all political persuasions: Americans’ growing
obsession with protein, and especially [animal-based
protein](https://www.numerator.com/resources/blog/protein-trends/).

But these reasons don’t quite hold up under closer scrutiny. When individuals
eat less meat, it [really does make a difference by reducing demand for
meat](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2022/4/22/23036010/eat-less-meat-
vegetarian-effects-climate-emissions-animal-welfare-factory-farms); Americans,
regardless of their political beliefs, [strongly oppose factory
farming](https://faunalytics.org/public-acceptability-of-standard-u-s-animal-
agriculture-practices/); and our fears of not eating enough protein are
[unfounded](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/410565/protein-muscle-gain-
weightlifting-plant-based-vegan) (and you can easily up your protein intake
with plant-based sources).

So as we think about what direction we’d like society to take in 2026, I hope
we can move past the surface-level, vibes-based dynamics that seem to
influence the public debate around American meat consumption, and rediscover
the airtight case that we really ought to eat less meat and more plant-based
foods.

If all that speaks to you, you can sign up for [Vox’s Meat/Less
newsletter](https://www.vox.com/pages/meat-less-newsletter-course) — a
practical guide to eating less meat and more plants. It covers questions like:

Moving to a more plant-rich diet is one of the most impactful New Year’s

## **The meat industry is probably way worse than you think (and not just for
animals)**

I don’t think it’s an exaggeration to call what we do to animals for their
meat, milk, and eggs a form of torture. It surely would be if it were done to
a pet dog or cat.

They’re bred to [grow so big, so fast](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/408152/animal-cruelty-factory-farms-chicken-welfare-genetics) that
many have difficulty walking, or have chronic joint and heart issues. Many
species’ [body parts are chopped off](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/419385/switzerland-meat-mutilation-anesthesia-milk-egg-label) — hens’
beaks, turkey’s snoods, cows’ horns, piglets’ tails and testicles — without
pain relief. Most hens and sows (female breeding pigs) spend their entire
lives in [tiny cages](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/353393/farm-bill-
republicans-prop-12-gestation-crates-pork), unable to move around. The vast
majority of farmed animals will never step foot on grass or breathe fresh air.
Many will [die prematurely from painful diseases](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/389639/chicken-poultry-industry-mortality-death).

This all happens on an incomprehensible scale — over 10 billion farmed birds
and mammals in the US and around 85 billion globally every year. If you count
farmed fish and crustaceans, which I certainly think one should — fish are
incredibly [underestimated and misunderstood](https://www.vox.com/future-
perfect/469054/fish-pain-debate-sentience-consciousness) — the global death
toll of animal agriculture gets close to one trillion animals each year.

The American livestock industry spends [a lot of
money](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/405341/mountaire-farms-poultry-
investigation-trump) lobbying politicians to keep things this way, and a lot
of money on advertising to [assuage consumers’
concerns](https://www.vox.com/22838160/animal-welfare-labels-meat-dairy-eggs-
humane-humanewashing).

To be fair, a tiny minority of companies and farmers treat their animals
better than the status quo, but it can be difficult to separate what’s real
from “[humanewashing](https://www.vox.com/22838160/animal-welfare-labels-meat-
dairy-eggs-humane-humanewashing),” and
[investigations](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2024/04/alexandre-
farms-treatment-of-animals/677980/) into some of the supposedly [highest-
welfare companies](https://youtu.be/yU4PJCuslD0?si=5mtoMAGKgBuIN-L5&t=405)
have exposed pretty terrible conditions. Seeking genuinely higher-welfare
animal products is a sensible response to the horrors of factory farming, and
it should be [part of the
solution](https://regressiontothemeat.substack.com/p/pasturism), but shifting
to a less-meat, more plant-based diet will have much more of an impact for
animals.

And the case for that dietary shift goes well beyond animal welfare. Consider
the following about meat and dairy production.

On the environment:

  * It’s the leading global cause of [deforestation and biodiversity loss](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/22287498/meat-wildlife-biodiversity-species-plantbased), because [so much land](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2023/10/20/23924061/public-grazing-land-cattle-meat-carbon-opportunity-cost) is cleared to graze cattle and grow feed crops for pigs, fish, and chickens.
  * In the US, it’s very likely the leading cause of [water pollution](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2023/8/31/23852325/farming-myths-agricultural-exceptionalism-pollution-labor-animal-welfare-laws) and a [top cause of air pollution](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/05/210510161505.htm), which has been a [blight](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/395967/iowa-factory-farm-pollution-sacrifice-state) on swathes of rural America.
  * It accounts for [14.5](https://www.fao.org/4/a0701e/a0701e00.htm) to [19.6 percent](https://thebreakthrough.org/issues/food-agriculture-environment/livestock-dont-contribute-14-5-of-global-greenhouse-gas-emissions) of global greenhouse gas emissions.

Meat’s social consequences:

  * Working in a slaughterhouse is [one of the most dangerous](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/405972/trump-slaughter-line-speed-poultry-pork) and [traumatizing](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/415294/slaughterhouse-meat-workers-ptsd-mental-health) occupations in the US.
  * Factory farming has led to [intense consolidation](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/22298043/meat-antitrust-biden-vilsack) in the meat sector, contributing to the closure of tens of thousands of small and mid-sized farms and the [hollowing out of rural economies](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2022/may/05/us-industrial-hog-farming-rural-towns).

It’s putting public health at risk:

  * Because disease spread is so rampant on factory farms, around 70 percent of _all antibiotics_ in the US and globally are used in animal agriculture — accelerating [antimicrobial resistance](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/401172/antibiotics-meat-pharmaceutical-industry-agriculture), which the World Health Organization has [called](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance) “one of the top global public health and development threats.”
  * Three out of four [emerging infectious diseases](https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/about/about-zoonotic-diseases.html) in people come from animals, and [increased meat production](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/24128700/bird-fludairy-meat-industry-h5n1-cows-milk-eggs-safety) is part of the problem.
  * While people can be perfectly healthy eating animal products, [America’s meat-heavy diets contribute](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/406933/maha-meat-dairy-rfk-dietary-guidelines) to our high rates of heart disease, cancer, and type 2 diabetes.

## **Make plant-based eating aspirational again**

What I find most empowering about plant-based eating is that, in a world where
we often feel powerless and overwhelmed, it’s something just about anyone can
do that tackles so many social problems at once. Plus, everyone already eats a
lot of plant-based foods; in the US, about [70
percent](https://www.ers.usda.gov/amber-waves/2016/december/a-look-at-calorie-
sources-in-the-american-diet) of our calories come from plant sources.

But getting started on shifting more of that 30 percent of animal-based
calories to more plant-based foods can be daunting. What should you eat
instead and how do you make new habits stick?
