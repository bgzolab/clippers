title: A good business vs. a useful idea
type: archive-web
---

Ideas open doors, lead to connections and make things better.

But not all good ideas are good businesses.

Crop rotation is a good idea. So is sous vide cooking and the sport of
juggling. But these aren’t good businesses.

A business thrives when it can charge a premium–selling something for more
than it costs. That means that there has to be a competitive advantage, an
asset that produces value. Easy substitutions are a challenge for businesses,
but useful ideas are often based on how easy they are to share.

Create something of value. And then find a reason why people will eagerly
choose your version and happily pay extra for it.
